\SOlevelB{Construction}\label{app:so:a1}

The generator delivers $f(x)=\sum_{i\in S}f_i(x_i)+\sum_{(i,j)\in
I}h_{ij}(x_i,x_j)$ on ${[0,1]}^D$ with $\Enu f=0$ and $\Varnu f=1$, together
with the ground-truth labels a posterior is scored against. Two quantities
carry the whole construction, and the package derives neither: the average
marginal variance $\vell{\ell}$ of the centered component kernel, which is
what turns an amplitude into a variance share, and the first-order ANOVA
decomposition, which is what a variance share \emph{is}. Both are derived
below from the definitions; the remaining subsections follow one draw through
centering, exact rescaling, pairing, rotation and maximization. Each
subsection closes by naming the function that implements its result and the
test that pins it.

\SOlevelC{Reference measure and quadrature}\label{app:so:a1:measure}

The reference measure is $\refnu=U{[0,1]}^D$, a product of $D$ copies of the
one-dimensional marginal $\refnu_1=U[0,1]$. Every mean, variance and Sobol
index below is under $\refnu$ unless it carries another subscript.

No one-dimensional integral is taken in closed form. Let
${(\hat t_q,\hat w_q)}_{q=1}^{64}$
be the $64$-node Gauss-Legendre rule on $[-1,1]$, mapped
affinely onto the unit interval by $t_q=\tfrac12(\hat t_q+1)$,
$w_q=\tfrac12\hat w_q$. The package integrates against the discrete measure
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:nuhat}
  \nuhat=\sum_{q=1}^{64}w_q\,\delta_{t_q},
  \qquad
  \sum_{q=1}^{64}w_q=1,
\end{equation}
the weights summing to one because the $[-1,1]$ weights sum to $2$ and each
was halved. Hence $\nuhat$ is a probability measure on $[0,1]$, and
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:quad}
  \Ehat\varphi=\sum_{q=1}^{64}w_q\,\varphi(t_q),
  \qquad
  \Varhat\varphi=\sum_{q=1}^{64}w_q\,{\varphi(t_q)}^2-{\bigl(\Ehat\varphi\bigr)}^2
\end{equation}
are exactly what \SOcode{kernel.nu\_mean} and \SOcode{kernel.nu\_var} compute
from a vector of node values. The hat marks the quadrature; the subscript
names the measure it approximates. A $64$-node Gauss rule is exact on
polynomials of degree at most $2\cdot 64-1=127$, so $\nuhat$ and $\refnu_1$
agree on that entire space --- but they are not the same measure, and the
residual difference is quantified in~\ref{app:so:a1:draw}.

Two conventions follow from this. First, every property this appendix calls
\emph{exact} is exact under $\nuhat$: $\Ehat f_i=0$ and $\Varhat f_i=s_i$ hold
by algebra, whereas the corresponding statements about $\Enu f_i$ and $\Varnu
f_i$ hold only to the accuracy of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:quad} on the integrand
at hand. The hat is never decorative and is never dropped. Second, a
two-dimensional block integral uses the product rule $\nuhat\otimes\nuhat$ ---
the $64\times64$ grid of node pairs with weights $w_q w_{q'}$ --- and $\Ehat$,
$\Varhat$ denote that rule as well; the dimension is the integrand's. Because
$\nuhat\otimes\nuhat$ is itself a product probability measure, every
product-measure argument below (Fubini, uncorrelatedness, the ANOVA
decomposition of~\ref{app:so:a1:rotation}) applies to it verbatim, with no
approximation of its own.

\SOlevelC{Centered kernel and $\vell{\ell}$}\label{app:so:a1:kernel}

Components are drawn from a Mat\'ern-$5/2$ kernel of unit amplitude,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:matern}
  \kl(x,y)=\Bigl(1+\sqrt5\,\tau+\tfrac53\tau^2\Bigr)e^{-\sqrt5\,\tau},
  \qquad
  \tau=\frac{|x-y|}{\ell},
\end{equation}
so that $\kl(x,x)=1$: the amplitude is normalized pointwise, not in any
average sense. That is the problem~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:vell} below fixes.

\textbf{Centering.} Following \SOciteOAK{} the kernel is projected onto the
orthogonal complement of the constants under $\refnu_1$,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:centered}
  \ktil(x,y)=\kl(x,y)-\Ex{z}\kl(x,z)-\Ex{z}\kl(z,y)+\Ex{z,z'}\kl(z,z'),
  \qquad z,z'\sim\refnu_1\ \text{i.i.d.}
\end{equation}
The reason this is the right object is one line: for $r\sim\mathrm{GP}(0,\kl)$
set $\bar r=r-\int r\,d\refnu_1$; then $\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(\bar r(x),\bar r(y)\bigr)
=\Ex{}\bigl[\bar r(x)\bar r(y)\bigr]$, and expanding the product
and exchanging $\Ex{}$ with the two path integrals (Fubini, the paths being
continuous and $\refnu_1$ finite) reproduces exactly the four terms
of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:centered}. So $\ktil$ is the covariance of the centered
path, and $\int\bar r\,d\refnu_1=0$ almost surely.

\textbf{The average marginal variance.} $\ktil(x,x)$ is not constant in $x$,
so ``the'' marginal variance of a centered draw has to be defined as an
average. Take $\Ex{x}$ of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:centered} on the diagonal. The
first term is $\Ex{x}\kl(x,x)=1$. The two cross terms are the \emph{same}
double integral --- averaging $\Ex{z}\kl(x,z)$ over $x$ gives
$\Ex{x,x'}\kl(x,x')$, and so does the mirror term, by symmetry of $\kl$ ---
and the fourth term is that same double integral once more. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:vell}
  \Ex{x}\ktil(x,x)
  =1-2\,\Ex{x,x'}\kl(x,x')+\Ex{x,x'}\kl(x,x')
  =1-\Ex{x,x'}\kl(x,x')
  =:\vell{\ell},
\end{equation}
which is \SOcode{kernel.v} verbatim: a single double sum subtracted from one.
The two subtractions collapsing into one is the whole content of the identity,
and it is why $\vell{\ell}$ can be evaluated without ever forming $\ktil$.

Divide by it. With $\bar k_\ell:=\ktil/\vell{\ell}$ --- so that $\kbar=\bar k_{\ell_i}$
is component $i$'s kernel --- we get $\Ex{x}\bar k_\ell(x,x)=1$ by
construction, and for $f_i\sim\mathrm{GP}\bigl(0,a_i^2\,\kbar\bigr)$: $\int
f_i\,d\refnu_1$ is Gaussian with variance
$\iint\kbar\,d\refnu_1\,d\refnu_1=0$, hence zero almost surely, so $\Varnu
f_i=\int f_i^2\,d\refnu_1$ and, by Fubini,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:unitvar}
  \Ex{}\Varnu f_i
  =\int\Ex{}\bigl[{f_i(x)}^2\bigr]\,d\refnu_1(x)
  =a_i^2\,\Ex{x}\kbar(x,x)
  =a_i^2 .
\end{equation}
This is the design brief's ``one-line change'' made explicit: without the
division by $\vell{\ell}$ the left-hand side would be $a_i^2\vell{\ell_i}$,
which varies over orders of magnitude across the lengthscales this study uses,
so ``the amplitude stays large'' would have no fixed meaning. With it, $a_i^2$
is the component's expected variance and $a_i^2/\sum_j a_j^2$ its expected
first-order Sobol index.

In code every $\Ex{z}$ in~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:centered}
and~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:vell} is the rule~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:quad}. The
identities therefore hold \emph{exactly} for $\nuhat$ --- which is what the
two orthogonality tests below check --- and for $\refnu_1$ to the rule's
accuracy on $\kl$.

\textbf{Large $\ell$.} Expand~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:matern} in powers of $\tau$.
Writing the exponential as
$e^{-\sqrt5\tau}=\sum_{m\ge0}{(-\sqrt5\,\tau)}^m/{m!}$, multiplying it by
$(1+\sqrt5\,\tau+\tfrac53\tau^2)$ and collecting terms, the coefficient of
$\tau^1$ is $-\sqrt5+\sqrt5=0$; that of $\tau^2$ is
$\tfrac53-5+\tfrac52=-\tfrac56$; and that of $\tau^3$ is
$\sqrt5\bigl(-\tfrac56+\tfrac52-\tfrac53\bigr)=0$. In the variable
$\varsigma=\sqrt5\,\tau$ the whole series reads
\[
  (1+\varsigma+\tfrac13\varsigma^2)e^{-\varsigma}
  =1-\tfrac16\varsigma^2+\tfrac1{24}\varsigma^4-\tfrac1{45}\varsigma^5
   +O(\varsigma^6),
\]
so
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:expansion}
  \kl=1-\tfrac56\tau^2+\tfrac{25}{24}\tau^4-\tfrac{5\sqrt5}{9}\tau^5
       +O(\tau^6).
\end{equation}
There is \emph{no cubic term} --- that vanishing is exactly why a
Mat\'ern-$5/2$ path is twice mean-square differentiable, and it is what
makes~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopekernel} below finite. The first odd power is
the quintic, and the leading correction to the rank-one limit is the quartic.

Keeping only the quadratic term,
$\kl\approx1-\tfrac{5}{6\ell^2}\bigl(x^2-2xy+y^2\bigr)$. Double centering
annihilates the constant and every summand depending on $x$ alone or on $y$
alone, and sends $xy$ to $(x-\tfrac12)(y-\tfrac12)$; the surviving term is
$-\tfrac{5}{6\ell^2}\cdot(-2)(x-\tfrac12)(y-\tfrac12)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:rankone}
  \ktil=\frac{5}{3\ell^2}\Bigl(x-\tfrac12\Bigr)\Bigl(y-\tfrac12\Bigr)
        +O(\ell^{-4}) :
\end{equation}
a rank-one linear kernel. Averaging its diagonal gives back the same limit,
$\vell{\ell}=\tfrac{5}{3\ell^2}\Varnu(x)+O(\ell^{-4})
=\tfrac{5}{36\ell^2}+O(\ell^{-4})$.

How fast is that limit approached? On ${[0,1]}^2$ the lag satisfies
$\tau\le1/\ell$, so for large $\ell$ the
expansion~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:expansion} may be integrated term by term
in~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:vell}. For $x,x'$ i.i.d.\ $\refnu_1$ the lag $|x-x'|$
has density $2(1-\cdot)$ on $[0,1]$, whence
$\Ex{}|x-x'|^n=2/\bigl((n+1)(n+2)\bigr)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:vasym}
  \vell{\ell}=\frac{5}{36\ell^2}
    \left(1-\frac{1}{2\ell^2}+\frac{4\sqrt5}{21\,\ell^3}+O(\ell^{-4})\right).
\end{equation}
The relative correction is algebraic of order $\ell^{-2}$, not exponential:
that is why the ratio column of Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_v_ell} climbs to
one so slowly. Equivalently, $\ell^2$ times the deviation of that column from
one tends to the constant $\tfrac12$, which is the $O(\ell^{-2})$ signature
of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:vasym}. The practical consequence is the one the design
brief flags: at the study's largest lengthscale $\vell{\ell}$ is smaller than
one by orders of magnitude, so a component's amplitude and its variance are
entirely different numbers, and only~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:unitvar} makes the
second of them the label.

\SOlevelC{Slope energy}\label{app:so:a1:slope}

The second sense of relevance the thesis contrasts is mean squared slope. For
a mean-square differentiable stationary process,
$\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(r'(x),r'(y)\bigr)=\partial_x\partial_y\kl(x,y)$, so
the slope energy is the mixed second derivative on the diagonal. Since $\kl$
depends only on $x-y$, differentiating~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:expansion} once in
$x$ and once in $y$ turns the quadratic term $-\tfrac{5}{6\ell^2}{(x-y)}^2$
into $+\tfrac{5}{3\ell^2}$, and the $\tau^4$ and $\tau^5$ terms contribute
nothing at $x=y$ because their second derivatives vanish at zero lag --- which
is the place the absent cubic term would have spoiled. Hence, exactly and
independently of $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:slopekernel}
  \Ex{}\bigl[{(r'(x))}^2\bigr]
  =\partial_x\partial_y\kl(x,y)\Big|_{y=x}
  =\frac{5}{3\ell^2}.
\end{equation}
Centering does not disturb this: it subtracts the random \emph{constant} $\int
r\,d\refnu_1$ from the path, so $\bar r'=r'$ pointwise. Dividing the kernel by
$\vell{\ell}$ divides the slope energy by $\vell{\ell}$. So for
$f_i\sim\mathrm{GP}(0,a_i^2\kbar)$, comparing
with~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:unitvar},
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor}
  \frac{\Ex{}\bigl[{(\partial_i f)}^2\bigr]}{\Ex{}\Varnu f_i}
  =\frac{5}{3\ell_i^2\,\vell{\ell_i}},
\end{equation}
free of the amplitude: a pure function of the lengthscale, tabulated in
Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_v_ell}. By~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:vasym} it tends to
$12$ as $\ell\to\infty$, and $12$ is exactly the slope energy of
$\pm\sqrt{12}\,(x-\tfrac12)$, the unit-variance linear function on $[0,1]$ ---
which is what~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rankone} says the component becomes. The
column descends towards that floor.

This is the design brief's contrast in its own terms. On the
\emph{unnormalized} centered kernel $\ktil$ at amplitude $a_i$ the two senses
of relevance read $\Ex{}[{(\partial_i f)}^2]=\tfrac53a_i^2\rho_i$ against
$\Ex{}\Varnu f_i=a_i^2\vell{\ell_i}$, with $\rho_i=\ell_i^{-2}$; their ratio
is again~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor}. Normalization moves $\vell{\ell_i}$
from the variance to the slope energy without changing which coordinate is
relevant in which sense, and~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor} is exactly how
far apart the two senses can be pulled.

The package goes one step further. The rescale of~\ref{app:so:a1:draw} makes
the variance share of component $i$ equal to $s_i$ exactly rather than in
expectation, so the \emph{theoretical} slope energy of the delivered component
is $s_i\cdot 5/(3\ell_i^2\vell{\ell_i})$ and the theoretical slope share is
that quantity normalized over the active set,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:slopeshare}
  g_i^{\mathrm{th}}
  =\frac{s_i\big/\bigl(\ell_i^2\,\vell{\ell_i}\bigr)}
        {\sum_{j\in S}s_j\big/\bigl(\ell_j^2\,\vell{\ell_j}\bigr)},
  \qquad i\in S,
\end{equation}
the factor $5/3$ cancelling. Two families are designed
around~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopeshare}: the anti-aligned family chooses
$(s_i,\ell_i)$ so that the $g_i^{\mathrm{th}}$ ranking is the reverse of the
$s_i$ ranking, and the decoupled family crosses strength against roughness so
that the two orderings disagree cell by cell; both are reported
in~\ref{app:so:a2}.

\SOlevelC{Draw and exact rescale}\label{app:so:a1:draw}

\textbf{The draw.} A component is drawn on the joint grid $Z$, the union of
$1024$ evenly spaced knots on $[0,1]$ with the $64$ quadrature nodes, so
$\knots=\SOnumKnotsUnit$ points with the nodes placed last (downstream code
slices the trailing $64$ rows, so the ordering is part of the contract). The
package factors $\kbar(Z,Z)=FF^{\!\top}$ by symmetric eigendecomposition, with
eigenvalues below $10^{-12}$ times the largest clipped to zero, and draws
$r_i=F\xi$ with $\xi\sim N(0,I)$; the covariance of $r_i$ is then $\kbar(Z,Z)$
exactly, up to that clip.

No jitter is ever added to the covariance (decision D3,~\ref{app:so:a4}). The
usual $10^{-8}$ ridge is independent white noise of standard deviation
$10^{-4}$ at every knot, and the stored component is a spline whose derivative
divides differences of neighbouring knot values by the knot spacing. That
contribution to the slope energy does not shrink as the component gets
smoother, whereas by~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor} the quantity it pollutes
is smallest for exactly the smoothest components. Clipping is free of that
defect: it removes energy from the numerically null directions instead of
adding energy in every direction.

\textbf{The rescale.} A single draw's variance is a random multiple of the
prior's, so the prior amplitude cannot serve as ground truth. Reading the
trailing node values, the package forms $\hat m=\Ehat r_i$ and $\hat V=\Varhat
r_i$ and stores
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:rescale}
  f_i=\mathrm{sign}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{s_i}{\hat V}}\,\bigl(r_i-\hat m\bigr),
  \qquad \mathrm{sign}\in\{-1,+1\},
\end{equation}
which is \SOcode{Component.from\_raw}'s \SOcode{scale} exactly. Two lines of
algebra, using only $\sum_q w_q=1$ from~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:nuhat}, give
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:exactshare}
  \Ehat f_i
  =\mathrm{sign}\sqrt{s_i/\hat V}\bigl(\Ehat r_i-\hat m\bigr)=0,
  \qquad
  \Varhat f_i=\frac{s_i}{\hat V}\,\Varhat r_i=s_i .
\end{equation}

\textbf{What ``exact'' means here.} Both statements
in~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:exactshare} are identities of the discrete measure
$\nuhat$, not of $\refnu_1$. They survive storage because the $64$ nodes are
themselves knots of the stored cubic spline --- the knot merge keeps the node
on a tie --- so \SOcode{Component.nu\_var} re-reads precisely the values the
rescale was solved from and returns $s_i$ to roundoff, with no interpolation
error in between. Under $\refnu$ itself there \emph{is} a gap: the
Gauss-Legendre error committed on a piecewise cubic, which is governed by
roughness, since $64$ nodes on the unit interval is only a few nodes per
lengthscale for the study's roughest components.
Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_quadrature_gap} measures it. Stated plainly: the
ground truth $\Varnu f=1$ is exact under $\nuhat$, and under $\refnu$ it holds
to the order the table's roughest rows report for the two families that
contain rough components (\SOcode{anti\_aligned} and \SOcode{decoupled}), and
to a far smaller order everywhere else. That is well below any effect this
study is powered to detect, but it is not zero, and the quasi-Monte-Carlo
$\Varnu$ columns reported for those two families in~\ref{app:so:a2} reflect it
rather than sampling noise alone; \emph{$\Varnu f_i=s_i$ is never written
without the hat}.

\textbf{Realized versus theoretical slope energy.} The rescale is exact for
the variance and therefore \emph{not} exact for anything else. Write
$\mathrm{SE}$ for the slope energy, $\mathrm{SE}(\varphi)=\sum_q w_q
{\varphi'(t_q)}^2$. Then~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rescale} gives
$\mathrm{SE}(f_i)=(s_i/\hat V)\,\mathrm{SE}(r_i)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:slopeinflate}
  \frac{\Ex{}\mathrm{SE}(f_i)}{s_i}
  =\Ex{}\!\left[\frac{\mathrm{SE}(r_i)}{\hat V}\right]
  \;\neq\;
  \frac{\Ex{}\mathrm{SE}(r_i)}{\Ex{}\hat V},
\end{equation}
because numerator and denominator are quadratic forms in the same Gaussian
vector $\xi$, and correlated. (Their ratio is bounded by the largest
generalized eigenvalue of the pair, so the left-hand expectation exists; it
simply does not factor.) The denominator is pinned exactly:
$\Ex{}\hat V=\Ehat\kbar(x,x)-\sum_{q,q'}w_q w_{q'}\kbar(t_q,t_{q'})=1-0=1$,
the first term by~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:vell} and the second because $\ktil$ is
doubly centered at exactly the nodes the double sum runs over. So the raw-draw column
of Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_slope_ratio} sits
on~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor}, and the rescaled column exceeds it by a
lengthscale-dependent factor.

The consequence is a rule for reading the labels, and it is not cosmetic.
\SOcode{Labels.g} is a \emph{realized} slope share, computed from the
delivered spline of one draw, and it carries draw-to-draw scatter of the
size~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopeinflate} implies. Any claim about a family's
\emph{design} therefore uses the theoretical
shares~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopeshare}, not the realized ones --- which is why
the anti-aligned family's acceptance criterion is stated on theoretical shares
(ruling R13,~\ref{app:so:a4}). The anti-aligned table of~\ref{app:so:a2}
carries two realized Spearman coefficients alongside the theoretical one: one
for the seed-mean realized $g$ against $s$, and one over all (seed, rank)
realized pairs. They differ precisely by this scatter, and~\ref{app:so:a2}
reads both.

\SOlevelC{Product interactions}\label{app:so:a1:interaction}

Non-additivity of the first kind is an explicit product term on a pair of
coordinates,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:hdef}
  h_{ij}(x_i,x_j)=c_{ij}\,u_i(x_i)\,u_j(x_j),
\end{equation}
where $u_i$ and $u_j$ are built exactly as in~\ref{app:so:a1:draw} but at
share $1$, so that $\Ehat u_i=\Ehat u_j=0$ and $\Varhat u_i=\Varhat u_j=1$.
Because $\refnu$ is a product measure and the pair's two coordinates are
distinct, Fubini applies to each of the following in turn.

\emph{(i) The conditional mean vanishes identically.} For \emph{every} $x_i$
in the knot range, $\Ehat[h_{ij}\mid x_i]=c_{ij}\,u_i(x_i)\,\Ehat u_j=0$ ---
not merely on average, and not merely at a node: only the $x_j$ integral is
discretized, and the factor it multiplies is a fixed number. Symmetrically in
$j$. So $h_{ij}$ has no first-order part at all, and adds nothing to any
$s_i$.

\emph{(ii) It is orthogonal to the main effects.} By Fubini,
$\Ehat[h_{ij}f_i]=c_{ij}\,\Ehat[u_i f_i]\cdot\Ehat u_j=0$. It matters which
factor does the work: $\Ehat[u_i f_i]$ is a generic non-zero number --- $u_i$
and $f_i$ are independent draws on the same coordinate, not orthogonal
functions --- so the orthogonality comes entirely from the \emph{other}
factor. A construction that accidentally made $u_i$ proportional to $f_i$
would still satisfy a test of \emph{(ii)} alone, which is why the test below
also pins $|\Ehat[u_i f_i]|$ away from zero.

\emph{(iii) Its variance is the coefficient squared.} $\Ehat h_{ij}=0$ by
\emph{(i)}, so $\Varhat h_{ij}=\Ehat h_{ij}^2=c_{ij}^2\,\Ehat u_i^2\,\Ehat
u_j^2 =c_{ij}^2\,\Varhat u_i\,\Varhat u_j=c_{ij}^2$. Hence the interaction
share is prescribed directly:
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:hshare}
  s_{ij}=c_{ij}^2,
  \qquad c_{ij}=\sqrt{s_{ij}},
  \qquad \gamma=\sum_{(i,j)\in I}s_{ij}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{The variance budget.} Main effects sit on distinct coordinates and are
centered, so any two of them are uncorrelated; an interaction is uncorrelated
with the main effects of its own two coordinates by \emph{(ii)} and with every
other main effect by independence and $\Ehat h_{ij}=0$; and two interactions
on disjoint pairs are uncorrelated because their product factors into four
centered expectations. Every cross term therefore vanishes and the variances
add:
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:budget}
  \Varhat f=\sum_{i\in S}s_i+\gamma,
\end{equation}
which every family sets to $1$. These identities hold under $\nuhat$ to
machine precision, not merely to quadrature accuracy, because the centering
that makes $\Ehat u_j=0$ was solved at exactly the nodes the quadrature sums
over; the residuals are in Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_orthogonality}. Under
$\refnu$ they inherit the gap of~\ref{app:so:a1:draw} and nothing worse.

\SOlevelC{Rotation}\label{app:so:a1:rotation}

Non-additivity of the second kind is a change of basis with no explicit
interaction term (decision D2,~\ref{app:so:a4}). On each of a set of disjoint
coordinate pairs $(a,b)$ the objective is evaluated at the rotated point
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:rotmap}
  \begin{pmatrix}z_a\\z_b\end{pmatrix}
  =\tfrac12+\Rtheta\begin{pmatrix}x_a-\tfrac12\\x_b-\tfrac12\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \Rtheta=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
every other coordinate passing through unchanged, and the pair contributes the
block
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:block}
  \phip(x_a,x_b)=f_a(z_a)+f_b(z_b),
\end{equation}
additive in $z$ but not in $x$. Write $c=\cos\theta$ and $s=\sin\theta$ below.

\textbf{The extended domain.} For $x$ in the unit square,
$\|x-\tfrac12\|_2\le\sqrt2/2$, attained at the corners, and $\Rtheta$ is an
isometry, so each rotated coordinate ranges over $\tfrac12\pm\sqrt2/2$.
Rotated components are therefore drawn not on $[0,1]$ but on
$[z_{\mathrm{lo}},z_{\mathrm{hi}}]
=[\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}-0.01,\;1-z_{\mathrm{lo}}]
=[\SOnumZLo,\SOnumZHi]$, the $0.01$ margin (ruling R7,~\ref{app:so:a4})
absorbing the few ulps by which a floating-point corner of ${[0,1]}^2$ can
overshoot the bound. The knot count is raised in proportion,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:next}
  \nExt=\bigl\lceil(z_{\mathrm{hi}}-z_{\mathrm{lo}})(n-1)\bigr\rceil+1
       =\SOnumNext
  \quad\text{at }n=1024,
  \qquad \knots=\SOnumKnotsExt,
\end{equation}
so the extended grid's spacing is no coarser than the unit grid's and a
rotated component keeps the resolution, and hence the effective lengthscale,
of an unrotated one. Contraction back into the cube was rejected for exactly
that reason: scaling the rotated square down to fit multiplies every effective
lengthscale by $|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|$, which would confound rotation with
smoothness and make the rotation sweep a smoothness sweep as well. Centering
and rescaling still happen under $\nuhat$ on $[0,1]$; only the knots extend.

\textbf{(i) The mean breaks.} $f_a$ is centered against its own coordinate's
$U[0,1]$ marginal, but by~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rotmap}
$z_a=\tfrac12+c\,(x_a-\tfrac12)-s\,(x_b-\tfrac12)$ is a linear combination of
two independent uniforms, so its marginal is the convolution of two uniform
densities of half-widths $|c|/2$ and $|s|/2$: trapezoidal in general and
triangular at $\theta=45^\circ$, where $|\cos\theta|=|\sin\theta|$. It is
supported on $\tfrac12\pm\tfrac{|c|+|s|}{2}$, wider than $[0,1]$ unless one of
$c,s$ vanishes, and it is not $U[0,1]$. Its mean is still $\tfrac12$, so a
\emph{linear} $f_a$ would still integrate to zero; a nonlinear one generically
does not, and $\Enu\phip\neq0$ away from $\theta\in90^\circ\mathbb{Z}$ (ruling
R30). The package computes the offset by the $64\times64$ product rule
of~\ref{app:so:a1:measure} and subtracts it once for the whole objective,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:mu}
  \mu=\sum_p\Ehat\phip .
\end{equation}

\textbf{(ii) The variance breaks.} $\kbar$ was normalized,
and~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:exactshare} was solved, on $[0,1]$ alone; the centered
kernel's marginal variance is larger outside that interval, because the
projection~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:centered} removed less there. So
$\Varhat\phip\neq s_a+s_b$ in general, and the objective must be renormalized.
Blocks are functions of disjoint coordinate groups under a product measure,
hence uncorrelated, so their variances add and one global factor suffices:
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:kappa}
  \kappa={\Bigl(\sum_p\Varhat\phip
               +\sum_{i\ \mathrm{unpaired}}s_i
               +\sum_{(i,j)\in I}c_{ij}^2\Bigr)}^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  f=\kappa\,(\Sigma-\mu),
\end{equation}
with $\Sigma$ the raw sum of blocks and unpaired components. (The third sum is
written for completeness and is always empty here: a rotation and an explicit
interaction may not coexist, ruling R39.) Three invariances follow and are
used throughout. Subtracting $\mu$ changes no variance,
so~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:kappa} may be solved before the recentering and needs
no correction after it. The \emph{location} of the maximizer is unchanged,
since $\kappa>0$ and the map is affine increasing. And the maximum transforms
as $\fstar=\kappa(\Sigma_{\max}-\mu)$.
Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_rotation_angle} shows how far $\mu$ moves and how
far $\kappa$ departs from one; the \emph{sign} of $\kappa-1$ is seed-dependent
--- rotation can move variance either way across the boundary of $[0,1]$ ---
as the count column records.

\textbf{(iii) The first-order shares, derived.} Under a product measure the
Hoeffding decomposition of a two-variable function is
$\phip=\Ex{}\phip+\bigl(\Ex{x_b}\phip-\Ex{}\phip\bigr)
+\bigl(\Ex{x_a}\phip-\Ex{}\phip\bigr)+\phip^{ab}$, the four terms mutually
orthogonal, so the variances add:
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:anova}
  \Varx{}\phip
  =\Varx{x_a}\Ex{x_b}\phip
  +\Varx{x_b}\Ex{x_a}\phip
  +\Varx{}\phip^{ab}.
\end{equation}
The first-order ANOVA term of coordinate $a$ is thus
$\Ex{x_b}\phip-\Ex{}\phip$, and its variance, rescaled
by~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:kappa}, is the axis-aligned first-order share
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:saxis}
  \saxis{a}=\kappa^2\,\Varx{x_a}\Ex{x_b}\phip,
\end{equation}
which is what \SOcode{rotation.rotated\_block\_stats} returns: it computes the
triple $\bigl(\Varhat\phip,\ \Varx{x_a}\Ex{x_b}\phip,\
\Varx{x_b}\Ex{x_a}\phip\bigr)$ under $\nuhat\otimes\nuhat$ and nothing else.
The remainder in~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:anova} is a variance and therefore
non-negative,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:remainder}
  \mathrm{rem}_p
  =\Varx{}\phip-\Varx{x_a}\Ex{x_b}\phip-\Varx{x_b}\Ex{x_a}\phip
  =\Varx{}\phip^{ab}\ \ge\ 0 .
\end{equation}
It vanishes when $\theta$ is a multiple of $90^\circ$. Away from those angles
it is strictly positive except in one degenerate case, which the algebra makes
explicit: from~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:block},
$\partial_a\partial_b\phip=cs\bigl(f_b''(z_b)-f_a''(z_a)\bigr)$, so additivity
in $x$ with $cs\neq0$ forces $f_a''\equiv f_b''\equiv\text{const}$ --- two
quadratics of equal curvature, which a Mat\'ern-$5/2$ draw is not, almost
surely.

At an \emph{odd} multiple of $90^\circ$ the pair comes out \emph{swapped}, and
this is the one place a reader is likely to get the identity backwards. At
$\theta=90^\circ$,~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rotmap} gives $z_a=1-x_b$ and
$z_b=x_a$, so $\phip=f_a(1-x_b)+f_b(x_a)$ and the first-order term of $x_a$ is
$f_b$, not $f_a$: $\Varx{x_a}\Ex{x_b}\phip=\Varnu f_b=s_b$ while
$\Varx{x_b}\Ex{x_a}\phip=s_a$. Only at an even multiple do the two come back
in order. A test on this branch once asserted the unswapped identity here and
passed, because both shares in its fixture happened to be equal.

Summing~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:saxis} over all coordinates and
using~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:kappa} for the total, the axis-misspecification
share is the rescaled remainder:
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:gammaaxis}
  \begin{aligned}
    \gaxis=1-\sum_i\saxis{i}
    &=\kappa^2\Bigl(\sum_p\Varhat\phip
      -\sum_p\bigl(\Varx{x_a}\Ex{x_b}\phip
                   +\Varx{x_b}\Ex{x_a}\phip\bigr)\Bigr)\\
    &=\kappa^2\sum_p\mathrm{rem}_p ,
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
the unpaired shares $\kappa^2\sum_i s_i$ cancelling term by term between
$1=\kappa^2\kappa^{-2}$ and $\sum_i\saxis{i}$. Keep $\gaxis$ and $\gamma$
distinct: $\gaxis$ is the variance a first-order additive model cannot see in
\emph{axis} coordinates, whereas $\gamma$, the explicit interaction
share~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:hshare}, stays $0$ for this family --- it has no
\SOcode{Interaction} at all. The rotated family is non-additive with
$\gamma=0$, and that is the point of it.

\textbf{The analysis rule (ruling R28).} The nominal angle does not index
axis-misspecification. $\gaxis$ vanishes at every multiple of $90^\circ$ and
is largest near the diagonal, so it is not monotone in $\theta$; the ranges at
the $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ rows of
Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_rotation_angle} overlap one another, and the
spread across seeds within one angle exceeds the gap between the medians of
adjacent angles. The study therefore conditions on the recorded $\gaxis$
label, never on $\theta$.

\SOlevelC{The maximum $\fstar$}\label{app:so:a1:fstar}

\textbf{Separability.} Interaction pairs and rotation pairs are disjoint, and
are checked to be so at construction, so the coordinates partition into paired
blocks, unpaired active coordinates, and coordinates $f$ ignores entirely.
$\Sigma$ is then a sum of functions of disjoint groups, its maximum is the sum
of the per-group maxima, and by the third invariance
of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:kappa},
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:fstar}
  \fstar=\kappa\Bigl(\sum_{\text{blocks}}\max\phip
                     +\sum_{i\ \mathrm{unpaired}}\max f_i-\mu\Bigr),
\end{equation}
with no joint search over ${[0,1]}^D$ anywhere (decision D1,~\ref{app:so:a4}).

\textbf{One dimension is closed form.} A component is a cubic spline, so its
derivative is piecewise quadratic and every critical point is a root of a
quadratic on its own knot interval. \SOcode{Component.argmax} takes the best
of those roots and the two endpoints; there is no grid and no iteration, and
the result is exact to floating point.

\textbf{Two dimensions are not.} A paired block has no closed-form maximum, so
it is scanned and then refined. An interaction block is scanned on the outer
product of its two components' knots restricted to the cube; a rotated block
is scanned on a uniform $1024\times1024$ grid \emph{in $x$-space} (ruling R8),
because a rotated component's knots live on the extended domain
of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:next} and are not the set $\phip$ is ever evaluated on.
The best cells are then refined by L-BFGS-B on the analytic gradient --- for a
rotated block, through the chain rule of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rotmap},
$\partial_a\phip=cf_a'(z_a)+sf_b'(z_b)$ and
$\partial_b\phip=-sf_a'(z_a)+cf_b'(z_b)$ --- and the best of the scan and the
refinements is returned, so the result can never be worse than the scan.

\textbf{Accuracy.} Re-optimizing from the returned point with far tighter
tolerances (\SOcode{ftol} $10^{-18}$, \SOcode{gtol} $10^{-14}$) and evaluating
the block afresh at the point the optimizer then returns does not improve the
value beyond floating-point roundoff on any paired block of the archived grid.
Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_fstar_reopt} is the source for how many blocks
and by how much; no count and no magnitude is restated here, and none should
be read from anywhere else. A gap measured against a finite reference grid
bounds the \emph{grid} rather than the method, since it shrinks as the
reference is refined, so no such gap is quoted here as this construction's
error.

\medskip \noindent\textbf{Sign convention --- read this once.} $\fstar$ is a
\textbf{maximum}, and simple regret is $r_t=\fstar-\max_{s\le t}f(x_s)$,
decreasing towards zero. The SAASBO implementation in this repository
\textbf{minimizes}, so a caller negates $f$ at the call site; the BoTorch
adapter's \SOcode{negate} flag does exactly that, and~\ref{app:so:a3:botorch}
states where. Nothing inside \SOcode{synthobj} is ever negated: $\fstar$,
\SOcode{Labels.x\_star} and every share in this appendix are stated in the
maximization convention.

\SOlevelC{Other generators}\label{app:so:a1:generators}

\textbf{Sinusoids, the one out-of-family check.} One study variant replaces
the Gaussian draw by a sum of a few random sinusoids, to test whether a
finding is an artefact of generating Mat\'ern components and then fitting
Mat\'ern kernels. To keep it comparable, the frequency is chosen so that slope
energy per unit variance matches~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor}. A
unit-variance sinusoid at $\omega$ cycles per unit interval has
$\Ex{}[{(u')}^2]={(2\pi\omega)}^2\Varx{}(u)$, since differentiation multiplies
the amplitude by $2\pi\omega$ and exchanges $\sin$ for $\cos$; equating that
to~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor} and solving,
\begin{equation}\label{app:so:a1:eq:omega}
  \omega(\ell)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{5}{3\ell^2\,\vell{\ell}}}.
\end{equation}
The identity behind it needs the averages of $\sin^2$ and $\cos^2$ over
$[0,1]$ to be equal and the mean over $[0,1]$ to vanish, which requires at
least one full cycle on the interval --- that is, $\omega(\ell)\ge1$. The
$\omega$ column of Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_v_ell} shows where the
crossing falls; above it, centering under $U[0,1]$ removes proportionally more
variance than slope energy and the realized ratio drifts above the target
(ruling R11,~\ref{app:so:a4}). The one variant that uses this generator sits
below the crossing.

\textbf{Monotone rejection.} The dense-weak family, which puts a share of
$1/D$ on every coordinate at a large lengthscale, additionally requires each
component to be monotone: it is the ``sparsity fails'' arm, and many weak
monotone coordinates are what the real tasks it stands in for look like. The
generator simply redraws until the grid values are non-decreasing or
non-increasing, which at that lengthscale is a cheap rejection --- the
rank-one limit~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rankone} says a very smooth centered draw
is close to a random multiple of $x-\tfrac12$, and a multiple of a monotone
function is monotone.

The rejection accepts \emph{either} direction, and that makes the extra random
$\mathrm{sign}$ of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rescale} redundant (ruling
R33,~\ref{app:so:a4}). The argument is one line: the law of a centered
Gaussian draw is invariant under negation, the acceptance region is symmetric
under negation, and the map
$r\mapsto\mathrm{sign}\cdot\sqrt{s/\hat V}\,(r-\hat m)$
of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rescale} is odd in $r$, since $\hat V$
is even and $\hat m$ is odd. So $(r,\mathrm{sign})\mapsto(-r,-\mathrm{sign})$
leaves both the law and the delivered component unchanged: the accepted draw's
direction is already a fair coin, and the component's distribution is the same
whether the coin is tossed or not. The coin is therefore not identifiable from
the stored values, which is why no sign is stored --- and why the test below
reads each component's realized direction rather than any recorded attribute.
