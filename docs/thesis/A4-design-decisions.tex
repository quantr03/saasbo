\SOlevelB{Design decisions}\label{app:so:a4}

The package was built under a decision ledger, and the entries below are the
subset of its rulings that changed the \emph{design} rather than the process.
Each is stated in three parts --- what the code does, why it does that rather
than the alternative that was rejected, and the function and test that pin it
--- so a reader can check every claim against the source.
Sections~\ref{app:so:a1} to~\ref{app:so:a3} carry the derivations, the
fourteen variants and the interfaces; this section carries only the reasons,
and cross-references them rather than repeating them. No entry below quotes a
measured number: the tables of~\ref{app:so:a1} to~\ref{app:so:a3} remain the
only source for those.

\SOlevelC{D1 --- the maximum is exact, and it is a maximum}\label{app:so:a4:d1}

\textbf{Decision.} $\fstar$ is computed, not estimated. Paired blocks ---
interaction pairs and rotation pairs alike --- are checked to be disjoint at
construction, so the coordinates partition into blocks
and~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:fstar} makes $\fstar$
a sum of per-block maxima: closed form in one dimension, where a component is a
cubic spline and \SOcode{Component.argmax} takes the best of the roots of its
piecewise-quadratic derivative and the two endpoints; and a scan refined by
L-BFGS-B on the analytic gradient in two, where no closed form exists
(\ref{app:so:a1:fstar}). No joint search over the cube happens anywhere. The
accuracy claim is the one~\ref{app:so:a1:fstar} makes and
Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_fstar_reopt} carries: re-optimizing from the
returned point with far tighter tolerances does not improve any archived paired
block's value beyond floating-point roundoff. No count and no magnitude is
restated here, and a gap measured against a finite reference grid is never
quoted as this construction's error, because such a gap bounds the reference
grid and shrinks as the grid is refined.

\textbf{Why.} Simple regret is $r_t=\fstar-\max_{s\le t}f(x_s)$, so $\fstar$
enters every curve the study plots. The rejected alternative was to estimate it
by a dense random or quasi-random sweep of ${[0,1]}^D$, which at
$D=\SOnumStudyD$ cannot resolve a maximum to anything near this precision and,
worse, would miss by a different amount in each family --- putting a
family-dependent floor under every regret curve and making the families
incomparable, which is the one thing a synthetic benchmark exists to avoid.
Separability is what makes the exact route affordable: it replaces one
$D$-dimensional search with a handful of one- and two-dimensional ones. That
the quantity is a \emph{maximum} is a separate decision, and the sign
convention it fixes is stated once in~\ref{app:so:a1:fstar} and once
in~\ref{app:so:a3:botorch}: the SAASBO implementation in this repository
minimizes, so a caller negates $f$ at the call site, but nothing inside
\SOcode{synthobj} is ever negated, and every share, every maximizer and every
$\fstar$ in this appendix is stated in the maximization convention.

\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{D2 --- rotation on an extended domain, renormalized and recentered}\label{app:so:a4:d2}

\textbf{Decision.} The rotated family's non-additivity is a change of basis and
nothing else: the objective stays a sum of one-dimensional components, but a
pair's two components are evaluated at rotated coordinates. Those components
are therefore drawn not on the unit interval but on the extended grid
$[\SOnumZLo,\SOnumZHi]$ of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:next}, with $\nExt=\SOnumNext$
draw points and $\knots=\SOnumKnotsExt$ knots --- the bound being the furthest
a corner of the cube can rotate, widened by the fixed $0.01$ margin carried in
the code constants \SOcode{rotation.EXT\_LO} and \SOcode{rotation.EXT\_HI}
(ruling R7). A rotated block's maximum is searched in $x$-space (ruling R8), on
a uniform grid over ${[0,1]}^2$ rather than on the components' knots. Rotation
then breaks two invariants, and both are repaired once for the whole objective:
the variance by the global factor $\kappa$ of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:kappa}, and
the mean by subtracting $\mu$ of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:mu} (ruling R30).

\textbf{Why.} Contracting the rotated square back into the cube was the
rejected alternative, and it fails for a reason that would have been invisible
in the results: it multiplies every effective lengthscale by
$|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|$, so the rotation sweep would also be a smoothness
sweep, and the two effects could never be separated afterwards. Extending
instead keeps the grid spacing no coarser than the unit grid's, so a rotated
component keeps the resolution --- and hence the nominal lengthscale --- of an
unrotated one; the margin exists only to absorb the few ulps by which a
floating-point corner of the cube can overshoot the bound. Searching in
$x$-space follows from the same extension: a rotated component's knots lie on
the extended domain and are not a set the block is ever evaluated on, so they
are the wrong search set. Recentering is not cosmetic, and the mechanism
is~\ref{app:so:a1:rotation}'s: each component is centered against its own
coordinate's $U[0,1]$ marginal, but a rotated coordinate is a linear
combination of two independent uniforms, whose marginal is trapezoidal on a
wider support and is not $U[0,1]$, so a nonlinear component generically
integrates to a nonzero value against it. Accepting the resulting offset was
rejected because $\mu=0$ holds exactly for every other family, and the rotated
family is the one the study compares against them: an uncentered mean there
would be a confound sitting in precisely the contrast the family exists to
make. One further consequence is an analysis rule rather than a design change,
and it is ruling R28: the nominal $\theta$ does not order
axis-misspecification. $\gaxis$ vanishes at every multiple of $90^\circ$ and is
largest near the diagonal, so it is not monotone in $\theta$,
and~\ref{app:so:a1:rotation} reads off
Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_rotation_angle} how far the seed-to-seed spread
within one angle reaches. No code moved for that one, because $\gaxis$ is
already a recorded label; the rule is simply that the sweep is conditioned on
the recorded $\gaxis$, never on the angle that generated it.

\SOlevelC{D3 --- fixed knots, clipped eigenvalues, and no jitter}\label{app:so:a4:d3}

\textbf{Decision.} A component is stored as spline values on a knot vector
fixed in advance: the sorted, deduplicated union of the uniform draw grid with
the $64$ Gauss-Legendre nodes of~\ref{app:so:a1:measure}, so the quadrature
nodes are themselves knots. The covariance is factored by symmetric
eigendecomposition with eigenvalues below a fixed relative threshold clipped to
zero; no jitter is ever added to the diagonal. Knots closer together than
\SOcode{component.KNOT\_TOL} are treated as the same point, and on an exact tie
the surviving knot is the \emph{node}: the merged values are sorted stably,
which places a grid point before a node of equal value, and the last entry of
each near-duplicate run is the one kept.

\textbf{Why.} Two independent arguments, one for each half of the decision.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{No jitter.} The usual ridge is not a small perturbation of the
    right kind. Adding it to the diagonal is exactly adding independent white
    noise at every knot, and the stored object is a spline whose derivative
    divides differences of neighbouring knot values by the knot spacing --- so
    the noise enters slope energy with a contribution that does not shrink as
    the component gets smoother, while by~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor} the
    quantity it pollutes is smallest for exactly the smoothest components. It
    would therefore corrupt the recorded slope-share labels worst where they
    matter most. Clipping, the alternative that was taken, is free of that
    defect because it \emph{removes} energy from the numerically null
    directions instead of adding energy in every direction; what it costs is a
    covariance that matches the target up to the clip rather than exactly, a
    bounded one-sided error confined to directions that carry no signal.
  \item \emph{The nodes are knots.} \SOcode{Component.nu\_var} is a quadrature
    sum over the node values, and the rescale
    of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rescale} is solved for that sum. Were the nodes not
    knots, the spline would have to be interpolated at them and the identity
    would hold only to interpolation error; because they are, the value the
    quadrature reads is the value the rescale was solved from, so the recorded
    share is exact under $\nuhat$ rather than nearly right. The dedup
    tie-break is not arbitrary for the same reason: keeping the node preserves
    that exactness, and in the one remaining case --- a grid point within
    \SOcode{KNOT\_TOL} above a node --- the quadrature reads the spline a
    tolerance away from the node, which moves it by far less than any label is
    quoted to.
\end{itemize}

\SOlevelC{D4 --- monotone components by rejection, and the sign that is not stored}\label{app:so:a4:d4}

\textbf{Decision.} The dense-weak family additionally requires every component
to be monotone, and it gets them by rejection: \SOcode{draws.draw\_until\_monotone}
redraws until the grid values are non-decreasing or non-increasing, accepting
\emph{either} direction. The explicit random $\mathrm{sign}$
of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rescale} is applied inside \SOcode{Component.from\_raw}
and consumed there. No field of any object holds it --- not on the component,
not on the labels, not on the objective --- and ruling R33 removed it from the
saved file format too, rather than adding a field to carry it.

\textbf{Why.} Rejection is used because at the family's large lengthscale it is
cheap: the rank-one limit~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:rankone} says a very smooth
centered draw is close to a random multiple of a linear function, and a
multiple of a monotone function is monotone, so the acceptance rate is high and
no shape-constrained construction is needed. Not storing the sign follows from
what accepting either direction does to it, and~\ref{app:so:a1:generators}
gives the argument in one line: the law of a centered Gaussian draw is
invariant under negation, the acceptance region is symmetric under negation,
and the rescale map is odd in the raw draw, so flipping both the draw and the
coin leaves the delivered component's distribution unchanged. The accepted
draw's direction is therefore already a fair coin, and the requested sign is
not identifiable from the stored values --- it is marginally unobservable, so
serializing it would record a quantity no reader could check and no
reconstruction could use. The rejected alternative was to add a field to
\SOcode{Component} or \SOcode{Labels} purely to serialize it, which is scope
paid for an unobservable. A reader who wants a direction reads the realized one
off the component's derivative, which is the honest quantity in either design.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{draws.draw\_until\_monotone} and
\SOcode{Component.from\_raw} (where \SOcode{sign} is applied and where it
stops). Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_draw\_until\_monotone\_returns\_monotone\_values\_at\_ell\_3},
\SOtest{test\_sign\_flips\_values\_and\_preserves\_the\_variance\_share} (the

\SOtest{test\_dense\_weak\_sign\_is\_not\_collapsed\_to\_one\_direction}, which

\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{D5 --- the sinusoid generator and the regime it is valid in}\label{app:so:a4:d5}

\textbf{Decision.} One variant replaces the Gaussian draw by a sum of a few
random sinusoids, and its frequency is not free: $\omega(\ell)$
of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:omega} is chosen so that slope energy per unit variance
matches the Mat\'ern generator's~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopefactor}. The identity
behind that match needs at least one full cycle on the unit interval, that is
$\omega(\ell)\ge1$, and \SOcode{draws.omega\_for\_ell} is kept as the closed
form regardless (ruling R11), with the pinning test parametrized only over
lengthscales in that regime.

\textbf{Why.} Matching the frequency is what makes the sinusoid variant an
out-of-family check on the generator rather than a change of two things at
once: the point is to ask whether a finding survives fitting Mat\'ern kernels
to non-Mat\'ern components, which is only a clean question if slope energy per
unit variance is held fixed. The
alternative --- a frequency chosen for convenience --- would have confounded
generator with roughness. Where the match does \emph{not} hold is ruling R23's
subject: the study's only sinusoid variant, \SOcode{aligned10\_sin}, sits at a
lengthscale whose $\omega$ column in Table~\ref{app:so:a1:tab:tab_v_ell} is
below one, so for that variant the match
is a design target and \emph{not} a realized equality: on the smooth side of
the crossing, centering under $U[0,1]$ removes proportionally more variance
than slope energy and the realized ratio runs above the target. The regime
restriction is therefore recorded on the test rather than hidden, and the same
statement is made about the variant in~\ref{app:so:a2:aligned10-sin}.
Consequently that variant's slope shares are read off the recorded label
\SOcode{Labels.g}, a realized quantity in the sense of~\ref{app:so:a1:draw},
and are never inferred from~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:omega}; the derivation and the
crossing are in~\ref{app:so:a1:generators}.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{draws.omega\_for\_ell} and
\SOcode{draws.sinusoid\_draw}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_omega\_for\_ell\_matches\_reference\_table},
\SOtest{test\_sinusoid\_ratio\_matches\_slope\_factor\_in\_derived\_regime}

\SOlevelC{D6 --- seeding by spawned sequences}\label{app:so:a4:d6}

\textbf{Decision.} One integer seed spawns three independent
\SOcode{SeedSequence} children --- one for selecting the active coordinates,
one for the component draws and one for the pairs --- and the draw child spawns
one further sequence per rank, so each component is drawn from a stream of its
own. Observation noise is drawn from a fourth stream, salted so it cannot
collide with any of them. The whole scheme is set out
in~\ref{app:so:a3:seeding}, which is the authority for it.

\textbf{Why.} The property being bought is separability: changing one part of a
\SOcode{FamilySpec} must not perturb a draw or a selection it has no business
touching. The rejected alternative --- drawing every rank in sequence from one
generator --- makes every component after the first edited one different, so no
variant-to-variant comparison at a fixed seed means anything, and a sweep would
be measuring reshuffled draws rather than the knob it varies. Spawning per rank
buys the opposite: at one seed, changing a rank's lengthscale leaves every
other rank's raw draw bit-identical rather than merely close. Ruling R29 fixes
the scope of that invariance, and the scope matters: it holds at fixed
$(D, \text{seed}, \SOcode{n\_active}, \SOcode{n\_pairs})$, where the same pairs
are produced whether they go on to carry interactions or to be rotated, so the
$\gamma$ sweep and the $\theta$ sweep each hold $S$, the pairs and the
underlying draws fixed and move only their own knob. It does \emph{not} say
that the two sweeps share $S$ or pairs with each other --- they are configured
with different \SOcode{n\_active} and \SOcode{n\_pairs}, and a permutation of a
different number of ranks is a different permutation. The correct form, with
the same qualifier, is stated in~\ref{app:so:a3:seeding}.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{families.streams}, \SOcode{families.select\_active},
\SOcode{families.select\_pairs} and \SOcode{families.noise\_rng}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_same\_seed\_gives\_a\_bit\_identical\_objective},
\SOtest{test\_changing\_one\_coordinates\_ell\_leaves\_the\_others\_bit\_identical},

\SOlevelC{R5 --- a deterministic archive}\label{app:so:a4:r5}

\textbf{Decision.} \SOcode{SyntheticObjective.save} writes the \SOfile{.npz}
through a hand-rolled zip writer rather than through \SOcode{np.savez}: every
member's timestamp is fixed at the zip format's earliest representable date,
the compression method is \SOcode{ZIP\_STORED}, and members are written in
sorted-name order. The
accompanying JSON is dumped with sorted keys. The same seed therefore produces
byte-identical files, and the manifest's \SOcode{sha256} values are stable
across regenerations.

\textbf{Why.} Byte-identity is the global constraint the archive is built to
satisfy, and the three knobs above are what \SOcode{np.save} itself --- already
deterministic for a given array --- does not control: a timestamp, a
compression method whose parameters could vary with the zlib build, and a
member order. The reason those knobs are not simply applied on top of
\SOcode{np.savez} is a layout difference: \SOcode{np.savez}'s writer opens each
member through \SOcode{zipfile}'s streaming, unknown-size-in-advance branch, so
every member carries a Zip64 extra field this writer's single known-size write
does not --- a more complex and more version-sensitive layout for no benefit
here, since every array's size is known before writing. That layout difference
is the whole of the case for writing the zip by hand, and no other property of
\SOcode{np.savez} is being argued against. What byte-identity does not survive
is a change of machine --- the stored values depend on the platform's BLAS, so
the hashes are local to the environment of
Table~\ref{app:so:a3:tab:environment}, exactly as~\ref{app:so:a3:files} sets
out.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{objective.\_write\_npz\_deterministic} and
\SOcode{SyntheticObjective.save}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_repeated\_save\_is\_byte\_identical},
\SOtest{test\_npz\_members\_and\_json\_keys\_are\_written\_in\_sorted\_order}

\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R13 --- the anti-aligned acceptance criterion}\label{app:so:a4:r13}

\textbf{Decision.} The family whose lengthscales reverse the variance-share
ranking is accepted on two assertions rather than on the per-seed one the
design first proposed. The first is deterministic and involves no draw at all:
the \emph{theoretical} slope shares of~\eqref{app:so:a1:eq:slopeshare} must
reverse the share ranking exactly. The second is realized but averaged: each
seed's recorded $g$ is aligned by rank, those rank vectors are averaged over
the study's seeds, and the Spearman coefficient of that seed-mean vector
against $s$ must clear a bound. The criterion is never evaluated on a single
seed.

\textbf{Why.} The per-seed criterion cannot hold, and it fails for a reason
that belongs to the design rather than to the implementation.
\SOcode{Labels.g} is a realized slope share computed from one draw, and
realized slope energy is a high-variance quantity (\ref{app:so:a1:slope}); this
family puts exactly \emph{one} coordinate at each lengthscale, so within a
single seed there is nothing to average over and one unlucky component inverts
a neighbouring pair of ranks. A criterion demanding the reversal at every seed
would therefore fail on correct code, which makes it a worse test than none.
The rejected alternative was to keep the per-seed bound and rely on the study's
own seeds happening to pass it: that is a threshold fitted to a lucky slice,
and it would not survive a change to the draw sequence. Splitting the criterion
instead puts the design claim where it is exact and the realized claim where it
is stable. Which realized statistic is meant needs saying precisely, because
two were once confused: the accepted one is the Spearman of the
\emph{seed-mean} rank-aligned $g$ against $s$, whereas
Table~\ref{app:so:a2:tab:anti-aligned} carries a third coefficient as well,
taken over all individual (seed, rank) realized pairs, which is a different and
weaker number --- the honest measure of how well a single draw reproduces the
design, and not the criterion. All three are read apart
in~\ref{app:so:a2:anti-aligned}.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.}
\SOtest{test\_anti\_aligned\_theoretical\_slope\_shares\_reverse\_the\_variance\_ranking}
(the deterministic half) and
\SOtest{test\_anti\_aligned\_realized\_slope\_share\_seed\_averaged\_is\_reversed},
whose assertion is on the seed-mean vector: it rank-aligns each seed by
sorting that seed's own $s$
descending --- \SOcode{Labels.g} is indexed by coordinate and every seed draws
a different $S$, so averaging by position would average incomparable quantities
--- and correlates the mean against the specification's shares.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R27 --- strict spec fields}\label{app:so:a4:r27}

\textbf{Decision.} The objective reads its four spec-derived fields through one
helper, and that helper applies a default only when there is no
\SOcode{FamilySpec} at all. A spec that is present must
carry every field the objective reads; a missing one raises
\SOcode{AttributeError} at construction rather than falling back.

\textbf{Why.} The rejected alternative was the obvious one --- read each field
with a default, so a partial spec still builds --- and it was rejected because
it was tried. A spec carrying plausible near-miss field names instead of the
real ones built without complaint and recorded the default noise level and the
default generator while the family name came through correctly, so the
resulting record looked entirely reasonable and was wrong about the noise on
every regret curve and about the scored ground truth of two variants. That is a
silent mislabel, the failure class this constructor exists to make impossible,
reintroduced by the very mechanism meant to be convenient. Failing loudly costs
a caller nothing, because a spec of \SOcode{None} still yields the same
defaults it always did.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{SyntheticObjective.\_spec\_field}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_spec\_none\_gives\_the\_custom\_defaults},
\SOtest{test\_spec\_fields\_are\_read\_when\_a\_spec\_is\_given} and
\SOtest{test\_a\_present\_spec\_missing\_a\_field\_raises}.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R29 --- membership, not a threshold}\label{app:so:a4:r29}

\textbf{Decision.} \SOcode{Labels.active} is a boolean mask of membership in
$S$ --- the coordinates that carry a main effect --- and not a comparison of
$s$ against a cutoff. The cutoff $\activeeps$ is still recorded, as
\SOcode{Labels.active\_eps}, so downstream scoring code reads one number
instead of re-deriving it, but nothing inside the package applies it.

\textbf{Why.} The two definitions come apart on the family built to make them
come apart. \SOcode{dense\_weak} spreads a share of $1/D$ over every
coordinate, so its shares fall below $\activeeps$ exactly when $D>1/\activeeps$
--- true at the study's $D=\SOnumStudyD$, false at the $D=\SOnumTableD$ the
fast tests build at. Had \SOcode{active} been the thresholded quantity, that
one family's label would have read all-True at one dimension and all-False at
the other, and a reader would have had to know $D$ before knowing what the
field meant. Membership is well defined at every $D$; the threshold is a
scoring choice, which is why it travels with the labels as a recorded number
rather than being baked into one. That \SOcode{dense\_weak}'s shares sit under
the bar at the study dimension is the point of the family --- it is the arm in
which sparsity fails, and nothing clears the relevance threshold --- and not a
defect in the label.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{Labels.active}, \SOcode{Labels.active\_eps} and
\SOcode{objective.ACTIVE\_EPS}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_active\_is\_membership\_in\_S\_not\_a\_cutoff},
\SOtest{test\_labels\_active\_matches\_S\_on\_the\_main\_objective},
\SOtest{test\_dense\_weak\_at\_D20\_has\_20\_components\_of\_share\_1\_over\_20}
and
\SOtest{test\_dense\_weak\_at\_D100\_share\_clears\_D20\_but\_not\_active\_eps},
which asserts both halves at once: the share is under the cutoff and
\SOcode{active} is all-True regardless.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R31 --- what the component-variance diagnostic reports}\label{app:so:a4:r31}

\textbf{Decision.} \SOcode{SyntheticObjective.component\_variances} returns
each component's, and each interaction's, pre-rotation variance under $\nuhat$,
excluding \SOcode{scale}. Under a rotation those numbers therefore neither sum
to the realized $\Varhat f$
--- which $\kappa$ forces to one --- nor give per-coordinate axis variances.
The ruling fixed the docstring and added a test; it did not change the return
value.

\textbf{Why.} The quantity is still meaningful ground truth: it is exactly
\SOcode{Labels.s} and \SOcode{Labels.s\_pairs}, measured rather than
prescribed, so it diverges from them precisely when one of them was not built
to the share it claims --- which is what a diagnostic is for. What was false was
the promise that it reports realized per-block variances summing to $\Varhat f$;
that holds without a rotation and only by coincidence with one. The rejected
alternative was to make it rotation-aware, and it was rejected on contract
grounds rather than on difficulty: the realized axis-aligned quantity is per
block, not per component, so the return value would change shape, and inventing
a new contract for a diagnostic mid-branch is how a caller silently starts
reading a different number. The wider quantity was already one field away, as
\SOcode{Labels.s\_axis}, so the fix was to say so.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{SyntheticObjective.component\_variances} and
\SOcode{Labels.s\_axis}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_component\_variances\_are\_the\_realized\_per\_block\_variances},
\SOtest{test\_component\_variances\_measure\_rather\_than\_echo\_the\_share} and
\SOtest{test\_component\_variances\_excludes\_rotation\_and\_scale}, the test
the ruling added.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R34 --- load restores the stored scale, offset and noise level}\label{app:so:a4:r34}

\textbf{Decision.} \SOcode{SyntheticObjective.load} reads $\kappa$, $\mu$ and
the observation noise level back from the file --- two of them off the stored
labels and one out of the JSON --- instead of re-deriving any of them from the
reloaded components and spec.

\textbf{Why.} The three were treated asymmetrically before the ruling: $\mu$
was restored, while $\kappa$ and the noise level were recomputed even though
both already sit in the file. Recomputation was verified to be bit-exact on
every variant, so there was no live bug; the objection is to the contract. The
stored $\fstar$ was computed with the stored $\kappa$, and \SOcode{\_\_call\_\_}
multiplies by whichever $\kappa$ the instance holds, so the moment the
computation of $\kappa$ changed --- a refactor, a different quadrature, a new
family --- a loaded objective would evaluate on one scale and report an
$\fstar$ on another, and every regret curve drawn from it would be quietly
wrong with nothing to fail. The same argument applies to the noise level, which
\SOcode{observe} reads. Restoring is also strictly simpler: it deletes a
computation whose result must already equal a value in the file. The rejected
alternative --- keep recomputing, since it agrees today --- trades a silent
future failure for nothing at all.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{SyntheticObjective.load}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_load\_restores\_scale\_from\_the\_file\_rather\_than\_rederiving\_it},
\SOtest{test\_load\_restores\_noise\_sd\_from\_the\_file\_rather\_than\_rederiving\_it}
and
\SOtest{test\_load\_reads\_mu\_from\_the\_file\_rather\_than\_rederiving\_it},
each of which corrupts the stored value and checks that the corruption is what
comes back, so a test that merely agreed with a recomputation could not pass.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R35, R36 and R37 --- the manifest describes the directory}\label{app:so:a4:r35}

\textbf{Decision.} The manifest is built by scanning the output directory after
generation, not from the pairs the invocation was asked to produce, so it
describes every objective present rather than the subset this run touched;
entries for stems it did not write are read back through
\SOcode{SyntheticObjective.load}. Its top-level fields describe the directory
too: \SOcode{Ds} is the sorted set of distinct dimensions across entries and
\SOcode{seeds} the sorted union of entry seeds, with the per-entry \SOcode{D}
and \SOcode{seed} authoritative, and entries in a canonical order. The exit
code answers one question --- is the manifest incomplete? --- so it is nonzero
only when something objective-shaped was excluded, an ordinary stray is warned
about but does not change it, and a dotfile is skipped in silence.

\textbf{Why.} A manifest that lists only the current run's output is a lie
about its own location: it sits inside the directory, so anything enumerating
the grid through it silently loses the stems an earlier, differently scoped run
wrote. The rejected alternative was to merge the new entries into the existing
manifest, which fails in the opposite direction --- it preserves entries for
stems that have since been deleted --- and scanning makes the choice between
rewriting and merging moot by construction. A scalar top-level dimension had
the same defect one level up: a directory holding two dimensions cannot
honestly report one, and the plural name is deliberate so a consumer cannot
read it as a scalar out of habit. The exit code's rule is the ruling that
corrects an earlier version of itself: making \emph{any} exclusion fatal was
tried and was a usability defect on this machine, because macOS writes
\SOfile{.DS\_Store} into any folder opened in Finder, so a routine run in a
perfectly good directory would exit nonzero and every script checking the code
would learn to ignore it. A signal that fires on every run is not a signal. A
dotfile is silent rather than warned for the same reason: a warning printed
every time is its own kind of noise.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{generate.\_scan\_directory},
\SOcode{generate.\_is\_dotfile}, \SOcode{generate.\_entry\_sort\_key} and
\SOcode{generate.main}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_manifest\_scans\_directory\_across\_differently\_scoped\_runs}
(the ruling's own scenario, two differently scoped runs into one directory),
\SOtest{test\_scan\_ignores\_dotfile\_silently\_and\_exits\_zero},
\SOtest{test\_scan\_warns\_but\_exits\_zero\_on\_ordinary\_non\_dotfile\_stray},
\SOtest{test\_scan\_exits\_nonzero\_on\_half\_written\_pair},
\SOtest{test\_scan\_exits\_nonzero\_on\_corrupt\_pair},
\SOtest{test\_scan\_exits\_nonzero\_on\_objective\_shaped\_pair\_under\_unknown\_variant\_dir}
and
\SOtest{test\_rerun\_manifest\_created\_changes\_but\_entries\_stay\_the\_same},
which separates the write timestamp from the entries it describes.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R38 --- where the BoTorch adapter applies the sign}\label{app:so:a4:r38}

\textbf{Decision.} The adapter declares
\SOcode{\_is\_minimization\_by\_default = False}. It overrides one evaluation
method, \SOcode{\_evaluate\_true}, and leaves the \SOcode{negate} flip to BoTorch's own
\SOcode{forward}; it does not flip the sign itself.

\textbf{Why.} The instruction it was built from said that \SOcode{negate}
flips the sign of \SOcode{evaluate\_true}, and that is false for the installed
library: in BoTorch 0.18.1, the version~\ref{app:so:a3:botorch} names,
\SOcode{evaluate\_true} returns the same values whether or not \SOcode{negate}
is set, and \SOcode{forward} is what applies the flip. Implementing the
instruction literally would have negated twice, and a double negation is
invisible in any test that only checks a magnitude --- it shows up as a
maximizer the optimizer runs away from. Following the stock pattern instead
means the flip happens exactly once, where a BoTorch loop expects it. The
maximization convention needs no special handling either: the
\SOcode{\_is\_minimization\_by\_default} flag exists in the base class for
naturally maximized problems and BoTorch's own bundled test functions of that
shape set it, so there is no library-versus-project conflict to reconcile ---
which is why the adapter is as thin as it is. What a caller sees is stated
in~\ref{app:so:a3:botorch}.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{botorch\_adapter.SyntheticObjectiveTestFunction} and
its \SOcode{\_evaluate\_true}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_optimal\_value\_and\_minimization\_flag\_follow\_our\_maximization\_convention}
and \SOtest{test\_negate\_flips\_an\_actual\_evaluated\_value}, which exercises
the flip through the call path rather than through
\SOcode{evaluate\_true}, because that is where it happens.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R39 --- rotation with an interaction is rejected}\label{app:so:a4:r39}

\textbf{Decision.} A \SOcode{SyntheticObjective} carrying both an
\SOcode{Interaction} and a rotation raises at construction with a message
naming the reason. No family in the study reaches the combination, and the
command line cannot produce it.

\textbf{Why.} Rejecting is the choice rather than computing the missing value,
and the reason is that no value is well defined. The label assembly skips every
paired coordinate when assigning an unpaired component's $\saxis{i}$, and
``paired'' there covers interaction pairs as well as rotation pairs, while only
rotation pairs get an $\saxis{i}$ assigned back. An interaction-paired
coordinate under a rotation would therefore keep $\saxis{i}=0$ and its true
first-order variance would be absorbed into $\gaxis$ --- a silent mislabel in
exactly the label the rotated family exists to report. Supplying a value
instead would mean deciding what an axis-aligned first-order share
\emph{means} for a coordinate that is both interaction-paired and rotated ---
real design work, and doing it late, under pressure, for a configuration
nothing needs is how invented semantics become silent errors. Refusing is one guard and is honest;
if the combination is ever wanted, it becomes a specified feature rather than a
quiet fix.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} The guard in \SOcode{SyntheticObjective.\_\_init\_\_} and the
$\saxis{i}$ assignment in \SOcode{SyntheticObjective.\_compute\_labels}. Pinned
by \SOtest{test\_rotation\_combined\_with\_an\_interaction\_is\_rejected} and
\SOtest{test\_rotation\_and\_interaction\_together\_is\_rejected\_at\_construction}.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{R4, R6 and R9 --- equality, missing values and tuples on the round trip}\label{app:so:a4:r4}

\textbf{Decision.} \SOcode{Labels}, \SOcode{Component} and
\SOcode{Interaction} are frozen dataclasses declared \SOcode{eq=False}, so
equality is identity. \SOcode{Labels.ell} is not-a-number off $S$, and the JSON
carries it as a bare \SOcode{NaN} token. \SOcode{load} converts the JSON's
lists back to tuples for every field declared as one, so a round trip
reproduces the original types and not merely the original values.

\textbf{Why.} A frozen dataclass generates an \SOcode{\_\_eq\_\_} that compares
its fields as a tuple, which on the array-valued fields here does not return a
boolean but raises on the ambiguous truth value of an array. Identity equality
is the honest contract in that situation --- the alternative, an elementwise
comparison written by hand, invents a notion of equality for ground-truth
labels that nothing needs, since the round-trip test compares field by field
anyway. Not-a-number off $S$ is chosen over a sentinel such as zero because a
lengthscale of zero is a value, and a reader who indexed by coordinate rather
than by rank would silently get one; the cost is that the JSON is not standard,
so a non-Python reader needs a not-a-number-tolerant parser. Restoring the
tuples matters because these dataclasses are the study's ground-truth record: a
field that came back as a list would compare unequal to the original, and
worse, would be mutable where the original was not.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{Check.} \SOcode{objective.\_labels\_from\_json} and
\SOcode{objective.\_spec\_from\_json}. Pinned by
\SOtest{test\_labels\_is\_frozen\_and\_compares\_by\_identity},
\SOtest{test\_component\_uses\_identity\_equality\_and\_is\_frozen},
\SOtest{test\_family\_spec\_shares\_and\_ells\_round\_trip\_as\_tuples} and
\SOtest{test\_round\_trip\_agrees\_with\_the\_original\_to\_1e\_15}, which
compares the reloaded labels field by field, arrays included, treating
not-a-number as equal to itself.
\end{sloppypar}

\SOlevelC{Not stored, not enforced}\label{app:so:a4:notstored}

Five things a reader may expect to find and will not. Each is deliberate, and
each is argued for above.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The requested sign.} Applied inside
    \SOcode{Component.from\_raw} and consumed there; no object holds it and
    the file format does not carry it. The realized direction is recoverable
    from the component's derivative; the requested one is not identifiable at
    all (D4).
  \item \textbf{An interaction factor's lengthscale.} The two unit-variance
    factors of an \SOcode{Interaction} are drawn at a lengthscale, but only
    their knots and values are saved, so \SOcode{load} rebuilds them with
    \SOcode{ell} set to not-a-number. The pair's share survives; its
    smoothness label does not.
  \item \textbf{The relevance cutoff $\activeeps$.} Recorded on every
    \SOcode{Labels} as \SOcode{active\_eps} and applied by nothing in the
    package. \SOcode{Labels.active} is membership in $S$ (R29).
  \item \textbf{Overlapping pairs.} Two blocks sharing a coordinate are
    rejected at construction, and the opt-in argument that names the case does
    not enable them either --- it raises, as not implemented. $\fstar$
    separates into per-block maxima only when the blocks partition the
    coordinates (D1).
  \item \textbf{Rotation together with an interaction.} Rejected at
    construction rather than given semantics (R39).
\end{itemize}
