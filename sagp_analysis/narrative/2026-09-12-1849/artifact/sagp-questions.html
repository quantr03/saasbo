Q0

What was compared, on what, and how was it scored?

narrative §1

One paragraph, verified against the code at commit a51a4b9 (sagp/gp.py, sagp/references.py, sagp/bo.py, sagp/diagnostics.py, synthobj/families.py) and against the manifests. D = 100 and T = 200 evaluations, of which the first 20 are a scrambled-Sobol initial design shared by the seven methods of a seed; observation noise sd 0.1; the acquisition is BoTorch's analytic LogEI maximized by optimize_acqf under Papenmeier's settings (512 raw samples, 5 restarts, perturbations around the best 5 % at sigma 0.001) [L7]. The 2x2 of cells crosses kernel STRUCTURE (a product or a sum of centered one-dimensional Matern-5/2 factors) with the SPARSITY PARAMETERISATION. The lengthscale cells put SAASBO's half-Cauchy scale mixture on rho_i = 1/ell_i^2, tau^2 ~ HC(0.1) and lambda_i ~ HC(1), so rho_i -> 0 removes coordinate i; the amplitude cells put the same mixture on the component variances a_sq_i with alpha = 0.0131, chosen so that both priors imply the same prior-predictive count of active coordinates, plus a LogNormal(0, 1.5) prior on each ell_i, and a_sq_i -> 0 removes coordinate i [L8]. Each fit is one NUTS chain, 512 warmup, 256 draws thinned to 16 retained, max_tree_depth 6, dense mass matrix; a fit is labelled "excluded" when split-R-hat > 1.1 or ESS < 16 on any sampled site or more than 5 divergences, but its draws are used to choose the next point regardless, so regret is valid for every run [L9]. References: sobol (the same Sobol sequence continued), dsp_map (Papenmeier's implementation of Hvarfner et al.'s dimension-scaled prior: an ARD Matern-5/2 at the MAP under LogNormal(sqrt2 + log(D)/2, sqrt3) lengthscales), and oracle_S (that same MAP GP restricted to the true active set through a FilterFeatures transform, so the acquisition still searches [0,1]^100). Families: aligned3 and aligned10 (3 / 10 active coordinates, equal shares, lengthscale 0.5); decoupled (8 active: shares 0.21 x4 and 0.04 x4 crossed with lengthscales 1.5 and 0.08, two coordinates in each importance-by-roughness class); interaction_g0.25 (5 active with main-effect shares 0.15 each, and two disjoint pairs carrying 0.125 each as product interactions) [L10]. Identification is scored at the last readout, t = 199, by ranking the 100 coordinates by the cell's native median (rho_i or a_sq_i, higher = more active in every cell) or by sobol_hat (first-order Sobol indices of the posterior mean under the uniform measure), and by the thresholded rule p_active > 0.5, where p_active is the fraction of the 16 retained draws with share > 0.02 (amplitude cells) or rho > 0.152, i.e. ell < 2.56 (lengthscale cells) [L11]. The brief's description of the study is correct on every point I checked; what it understates is the degenerate mode of section 5a, which is neither confined to two seeds nor to the last readout.

Q1

Which surrogate optimizes best, and does the answer depend on the objective's structure?

Short answer, in the narrative's words

The cell whose kernel structure matches the objective wins the optimization, by a paired margin that 10 seeds can establish: the additive cells on the two additive families, the product cells once a quarter of the variance sits in pairwise interactions, and every cell beats quasi-random search in every seed of every family [L1].

What can a paired 10-seed comparison establish? narrative §2

What a paired 10-seed comparison can and cannot establish. Pairing removes the objective-to-objective variation, which spans many orders of magnitude within a family: on aligned3 a single method's final regret spans 1e-5 to 0.21 (product-lengthscale) or 1e-9 to 0.017 (product-amplitude) across seeds [L12]. The price is resolution. With 10 seeds the smallest two-sided p is 0.002 (all 10 differences of one sign); 9/10 gives a sign-test p of 0.021 and an exact 95 % interval on the win probability of [0.55, 1.00]; 8/10 gives p = 0.11 and [0.44, 0.97], which contains a coin flip [L13]. So a 10/10 sweep is strong evidence of a consistent direction (win probability at least 0.69), a 9/10 result is evidence, and 8/10 or fewer is not, whatever the medians say. 72 such tests were run (12 cell-vs-reference and 6 cell-vs-cell per family, 4 families), so at p < 0.05 about 3.6 false positives are expected if nothing were real; the Holm column in the table is the reference correction and is not applied to the headline values [L14]. Paired tests also say nothing about the size of a difference on a scale that transfers to other problems: the medians below are on the regret scale of these particular draws. Rule used below and in section 6: SUPPORTED = at least 9/10 seeds and p <= 0.01; SUGGESTIVE = the median favours the claim and (p <= 0.05 or at least 8/10 seeds) but not SUPPORTED; NOT SUPPORTED otherwise.

Told by family: where does each cell win? narrative §2

Everything here is the paired final regret at t = 199 (regret = f_star − best_f, lower is better), the median over the 10 seeds of a family, and the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test over seeds from paired_wilcoxon_final_regret.csv [analysis: analyze.py, paired_tests].

Why does product-lengthscale lose to additive-amplitude in every seed of both additive families? narrative §2

Why product-lengthscale loses to additive-amplitude in every seed of both additive families [inference; confidence high on the direction, medium on the mechanism]. Three observations line up. (i) The gap opens early and persists: at t = 100 product-lengthscale's median is 0.48 on aligned10 and 0.54 on decoupled, additive-amplitude's 0.18 and 0.20 [L21, L32]. (ii) Product-lengthscale's own readout sees only about half of the active set on these families: thresholded recall 0.53 (aligned10) and 0.56 (decoupled), with the median rho on the true coordinates at 0.19 and 0.29 against the active cutoff 0.152 and a 10th percentile of 0.0007 and 0.0012 [L33]. The additive-lengthscale cell, same prior and other structure, has recall 0.90 and 0.79 with median rho 2.5 and 3.0 on the same coordinates [L34]. (iii) On decoupled the product cell's misses concentrate on the low-share coordinates (detected 20 % and 35 % of the time, against 65 % and 55 % for additive-lengthscale) [L35]. The mechanism I take from this: an additive kernel learns each f_i from the projection of all n points onto coordinate i, so its data requirement grows with the number of active coordinates, whereas an ARD product kernel must resolve the joint 10-dimensional function, and 200 points do not suffice; the SAAS prior then does what it is built to do and shrinks the lengthscales it cannot pin down, which removes real coordinates from the model, and the acquisition optimizes the coordinates that remain. The additive-amplitude cell also has poor thresholded recall on aligned10 (0.51) [L36], but its shrinkage acts on amplitudes and leaves the component shapes in place (section 3), so its posterior mean still moves in every active coordinate. Product-lengthscale's failures are also bimodal across seeds: on aligned10 four seeds end below 0.1, one at 0.22 and five between 0.36 and 0.79 [L37], with no relation to that run's identification score (Spearman +0.24 over 10 runs [L38]).

Why do the additive cells fall behind once a quarter of the variance is interaction, and only match oracle_S? narrative §2

Why the additive cells fall behind once a quarter of the variance is interaction, and only match oracle_S [inference; confidence high]. An additive model of f = sum_i f_i + c u_i u_j + c u_k u_l can represent the main effects but sees the two interaction blocks (0.125 of the variance each [L10]) as noise it cannot reduce, plus a spurious main effect it can invent: at t = 199, 6 of 10 additive-lengthscale runs and 5 of 10 additive-amplitude runs on this family declare one inactive coordinate active, against none for the product cells [L39] (section 5c). Its posterior mean is the additive projection of f, whose maximizer is not f's; the regret floor of 0.017 to 0.021 [L26] is the price of that projection. The product cells represent the interaction natively and reach 0.0007 to 0.0013 [L26]. oracle_S, an ARD Matern on the five true coordinates, is a full-interaction model on the right subspace and so the natural benchmark here; the additive cells tie it [L28]. That the additive cells still beat dsp_map twenty-fold in median [L26] says that at D = 100 and this budget a wrong-but-sparse structural prior is worth more than a right-but-vague one.

Q2

Do the cells recover the true active set?

Short answer, in the narrative's words

All four cells recover the true active set as a ranking (pooled average precision 0.89 to 0.97) [L2], but the thresholded rule "p_active > 0.5" is not a calibrated active-set decision in the amplitude cells: their NUTS chains never pass the convergence gate [L3], and the share threshold is applied in units that the BO design has deflated by a factor of about two [L4].

Scores are at the last readout (t = 199). AP is the average precision of ranking the 100 coordinates by a score; recall@|S| is the fraction of S inside the top |S|; the thresholded metrics use p_active > 0.5 [analysis: analyze.py, identification].

As a ranking? narrative §3

Ranking works in every cell [observation]. Pooled over families, AP by native_median is product-amplitude 0.965, product-lengthscale 0.952, additive-amplitude 0.941 and additive-lengthscale 0.892 (dsp_map 0.677); by sobol_hat it is 0.976, 0.935, 0.958 and 0.961 (dsp_map 0.590) [L40]. The median AP over runs is 1.0 in all four cells [L41]: the typical run ranks S perfectly and the means are pulled down by a few seeds. The cross-family pattern mirrors the regret: additive-lengthscale is the best ranker on aligned10 and decoupled (AP 0.979 and 0.971) and product-lengthscale on interaction_g0.25 and aligned3 (AP 1.00 in every seed, tied with product-amplitude) [L42]. Additive-lengthscale's pooled figure is dragged down by aligned3 (AP 0.806) [L43], entirely by the two collapsed seeds of section 5a; its other eight aligned3 seeds are at AP 1.0 [L44].

As a thresholded decision? And if not, is it the prior, the chain, or the threshold? narrative §3

The thresholded rule is precise but conservative, and in the amplitude cells it is not a decision rule at all [observation, then inference]. Precision at p_active > 0.5 is 0.92 to 1.00 in every cell [L45], but recall is 0.25 (product-amplitude) and 0.51 (additive-amplitude) on aligned10, and 0.39 and 0.54 on decoupled [L46]; the lengthscale cells sit at 0.53 / 0.90 (aligned10) and 0.56 / 0.79 (decoupled) [L47]. The question posed was whether this is shrinkage from the amplitude prior, the poorly mixed chain, or the threshold itself. All three contribute, and the tables separate them in part:

  • The threshold is applied in the wrong units [observation plus inference; confidence high]. A draw counts as active when its share a_sq_i / (1 − noise) exceeds 0.02, and 0.02 was chosen against the labels' shares, which are variances under the uniform measure of an objective with unit variance. But the cells are fitted to y standardized over the BO design, and that design is not uniform: at t = 199 the standardization std averages 1.52 to 1.56 on aligned10, 1.33 to 1.40 on decoupled, 1.34 to 1.56 on interaction_g0.25 and 0.96 to 1.10 on aligned3, across the four cells [L48]. In the model's units a true share s_i is therefore about s_i / y_std^2: a 0.10 share on aligned10 becomes 0.042, a 0.04 share on decoupled becomes 0.020, which is the threshold itself, and a 0.21 share becomes 0.106 [L49]. The four low-share decoupled coordinates cannot be detected by this rule with any sampler, and the aligned10 coordinates sit within a factor of two of the cutoff.
  • On top of that, the posterior medians sit below even the deflated value [observation]. The median a_sq on the true coordinates is 0.021 (additive-amplitude) and 0.011 (product-amplitude) on aligned10 against the deflated 0.042 [L50]; on decoupled the additive-amplitude medians are 0.038 (high-share smooth), 0.079 (high-share rough), 0.010 and 0.012 (low-share) against deflated 0.106 and 0.020 [L51]. On aligned3 and interaction_g0.25, where the shares are larger, additive-amplitude is at 0.83x and 1.35x the deflated value, so the shortfall is specific to the many-small-shares families [L52]. The product-amplitude cell's a_sq is not a first-order share (its kernel's variance is prod(1 + a_sq_i) − 1, so with many active coordinates each a_sq_i must be smaller than its share), which explains part of its shortfall; the additive-amplitude cell's factor of two on aligned10 has no such excuse. Whether that is the prior (alpha = 0.013 on the global scale [L8]) or sampler bias cannot be separated with one chain and 10 seeds [hypothesis: both, since the shortfall is largest exactly where the gate diagnostics are worst].
  • The chain makes p_active coarse and noisy [observation]. p_active is a fraction of 16 retained draws from a chain with about 6 to 7 effective draws on the a_sq site [L53]; on the true aligned10 coordinates its 10th to 90th percentile range is 0.00 to 0.94 for additive-amplitude [L54]. It behaves like a coin toss on whether a chain that has not mixed sat above or below the cutoff.
  • Ranking survives all three because the true coordinates' a_sq (0.011 to 0.03 in median) still exceed the largest inactive a_sq by one to two orders of magnitude (median over runs of the off-S maximum: 0.0003 to 0.001 on aligned10 and decoupled) [L55]. AP measures the gap; the threshold happens to sit inside it.

Does the neutral readout agree with the cell's own, and does the native score order by importance or by roughness? narrative §3

Three readings. (i) Every cell places the four high-share coordinates in its top eight at least 95 % of the time and the low-share ones 70 to 90 % of the time, so ranking by importance works in all four [L59]. (ii) The lengthscale cells' native score orders by roughness, as its units dictate: additive-lengthscale's median rho is 120 on the high-share rough class (ell 0.09; the true value is 0.08) against 2.2 on the high-share smooth class (ell 0.67; true 1.5), and its median native rank is 1.5 for high-rough against 5.0 for high-smooth at the same true share [L60]. (iii) The amplitude cells' native score orders by importance but roughness leaks in: additive-amplitude's median a_sq is 0.079 on high-rough against 0.038 on high-smooth, the same true share, and product-amplitude detects high-smooth coordinates only 45 % of the time against 95 % for high-rough [L61]. [hypothesis for the leak] A smooth component varies little across a design concentrated near the optimum and can be partly absorbed by the constant mean and by neighbouring components, so its amplitude is underestimated; a rough component cannot be absorbed. The neutral readout does what it was built for: sobol_hat's medians are 0.20 and 0.26 on the two high-share classes and 0.040 and 0.037 on the low-share ones for additive-lengthscale, and 0.23 / 0.20 and 0.037 / 0.032 for additive-amplitude, against true shares 0.21 and 0.04 [L62]. So on decoupled the answer to "does the neutral readout agree with the cell's own" is: on membership yes, on order no for the lengthscale cells, and sobol_hat is the readout to report as a share estimate. dsp_map, by contrast, places the high-share smooth coordinates in its top eight only 35 % of the time and the low-share smooth ones never [L63]: a MAP ARD lengthscale is blind to smooth components.

When does each cell lock on? narrative §3

When each cell locks on [observation] (median AP by native_median across seeds at the readouts t = 20, 25, 50, ..., 175, 199 [L56]). aligned3: all four cells reach AP 1.0 and F1 1.0 at t = 50 and stay there. aligned10: additive-lengthscale reaches AP >= 0.9 at t = 125 and F1 >= 0.8 at 125 (F1 1.0 at 199); additive-amplitude and product-amplitude reach AP 0.9 at 175, product-lengthscale at 199; no amplitude cell and not product-lengthscale ever reaches median F1 0.8. decoupled: additive-lengthscale at 150 (F1 0.86 at 199), product-amplitude 175, additive-amplitude and product-lengthscale 199. interaction_g0.25: product-lengthscale at 100, product-amplitude 125, additive-amplitude 199; additive-lengthscale never, because its median AP peaks at 0.89 at t = 125 and falls to 0.78 by t = 199 while its F1 holds at 0.91 [L57], the false-positive creep of section 5c. The structure-matched cell identifies first, by 50 to 75 iterations, and the last-readout scores are still rising at t = 199 for the amplitude cells on aligned10 and decoupled, so 200 evaluations is not a saturated budget for identification there.

Does identification predict optimization? narrative §3

Does identification predict optimization? Within a family and cell, the Spearman correlation between a run's final AP and its final regret is mostly negative but weak (−0.65 to +0.24 over 10 runs) [L38]; nothing at n = 10 is significant, and the two should be reported separately rather than as proxies for each other.

What survives, for the amplitude cells.

  • Regret survives [inference; confidence high]. The loop queried with the draws it had, so the regret is what the method actually did; an unconverged posterior is a worse surrogate, and if anything the amplitude cells are handicapped, not favoured, by their sampler.
  • Ranking by native_median survives as an empirical fact [observation]: pooled AP 0.94 and 0.97 [L40], because it needs only the order of a_sq across coordinates and the on-S / off-S gap is one to two orders of magnitude [L55].

Q4

What looks wrong, and must be fixed before a paper claims anything about the readouts?

Short answer, in the narrative's words

Two things look wrong and should be fixed before a paper claims anything about the readouts: the additive-lengthscale cell flips intermittently into a mode that declares all 100 coordinates active on aligned3 (5 of 10 seeds, from about t = 75 on, invisible to single-chain R-hat) [L5], and oracle_S is a weak reference because a MAP ARD GP cannot exploit additivity and spends between 8 and 24 % of its late queries on near-duplicates of its incumbent [L6].

Why does additive-lengthscale flip into an all-active mode on aligned3, and why this cell? narrative §5a

[observation] The numeric report flags seeds 02 and 08 at t = 199: 100 of 100 coordinates with p_active > 0.5, AP by rho 0.02 and 0.04, AP by sobol_hat 1.00 and 0.92, regret 0.0012 and 0.00002 [L72]. Scanning every readout of every cell run (200 runs x 180 readouts) [L73]: the same mode appears in five of the ten aligned3 additive-lengthscale seeds, 01, 02, 05, 08 and 09, in 25, 23, 27, 70 and 54 of their 180 readouts, first at t = 83, 87, 73, 80 and 85. It never appears before t = 73, and it is intermittent: seed 02 alternates, with 100 coordinates active at t = 150, 3 at 160, 100 at 170, 3 at 175, 100 at 185, 3 at 190 and 100 at 199 [L74]. Seeds 01, 05 and 09 happened to end in the sparse mode at t = 199, which is why a last-readout score saw two seeds. Outside aligned3 the mode occurs in exactly one readout each of decoupled seeds 01 and 05 (95 and 79 coordinates active at t = 94 and 49) and nowhere in the other three cells [L75]. The dsp_map hits in the same scan are a different and expected artefact: a MAP whose lengthscales stay at the prior mode, ell = 2.05 < 2.56, early in a run, counts as all-active under the rho rule [L76].

[observation] The retained draws say what the mode is [L77, L78]. In the sparse mode (seed 08, t = 75) the 16 draws have outputscale 0.48, noise 0.0049, tau^2 7e-5, rho 2.3 on S (ell 0.66) and 7e-5 off S (ell 123). In the collapsed mode (t = 100) all 16 draws have 98 to 100 coordinates above the cutoff; the outputscale is 140x smaller at 0.0034, tau^2 is a million-fold larger at 82, rho is 167 on S and 121 off S (ell 0.077 and 0.091), and the noise is 0.014, about three times the sparse mode's. The true coordinates are not the roughest in that mode: at t = 199 their median rho is 31 against 38 off S, so ranking by rho is scrambled rather than cleanly inverted [L79]. The collapse begins only after the run has converged: seed 08's regret has been 0.000019 since t = 60 and seed 02's 0.00115 since t = 40 [L80].

[inference] This is a second posterior mode of the additive-lengthscale model, in which every coordinate carries a low-amplitude, short-lengthscale component and the global scale absorbs the difference. Each iteration runs a fresh single chain, so which mode a readout reports is a lottery with a late-run probability of roughly 0.2 to 0.4 in the affected seeds [L73]. The gate is blind to it within a chain: six of the seven fully collapsed readouts I inspected have status ok with r_hat_max between 1.04 and 1.10, and the seventh failed marginally at 1.108 [L81]; it catches only the transitions, where the retained draws straddle the modes (seed 02 at t = 150 and 199: 12 of 16 draws collapsed, r_hat 1.46 and 1.56, 30 and 22 divergences) [L82]. The sobol_hat readout is right in both modes because the posterior mean's variance under the uniform measure still comes from the three components that track f [L72], and the regret is unaffected because the loop had converged before the mode appeared [L80].

[hypothesis; medium confidence] Why this cell and not the others. sagp/gp.py documents that the additive-lengthscale kernel is deliberately not normalized ("sparsity is rho_i -> 0, which drives v(ell_i) and the component to zero, so dividing v out would undo the shrinkage") and, for the amplitude cells, that the LogNormal(0, 1.5) prior on ell was put there to discourage "the ell -> 0 corner where a normalized component degenerates into white noise and competes with noise", adding that "the lengthscale cells have none: there the half-Cauchy on rho is the lengthscale prior" [L83]. So the designers anticipated exactly this degeneracy, guarded the amplitude cells against it, and left the lengthscale cells to the half-Cauchy. In the additive-lengthscale kernel a coordinate with ell -> 0 contributes an additive term that is nearly white on distinct points, which the likelihood can trade against the noise site; in the product-lengthscale kernel the same limit kills every correlation and is heavily penalised, which is why the product cell has no such mode. Why it appears only after convergence, and only on aligned3, I cannot settle from these tables; one candidate is that a design whose late points are near-constant in y favours a small global scale with many rough components. I checked and rejected one alternative: the late queries of the additive-lengthscale cell are not near-duplicates of earlier ones (0 % within 0.01 in max-norm on aligned3 [L84]), so this is not an artefact of repeated points.

[what to do] Score identification by sobol_hat or by a readout aggregated over several iterations, not by the last rho readout; and run 4 short chains instead of 1 in the sampler pilot of section 7, which would make this mode visible as an R-hat far above 1.1.

Is oracle_S a fair oracle? narrative §5b

[observation] oracle_S loses to additive-amplitude in 10/10 seeds on aligned10 (median 0.43 against 0.027) and on decoupled (0.44 against 0.058), and to additive-lengthscale 9/10 on both [L85]. On aligned3 it is the worst model-based method (median 0.0033, beaten 9/10 by each amplitude cell) [L86]. On interaction_g0.25 it is competitive: third, tied with the additive cells and beaten 8/10 by product-lengthscale [L87].

[inference; confidence high] The oracle knows S but fits one ARD Matern-5/2 GP at the MAP on those coordinates. On an additive objective of 10 coordinates that is a 10-dimensional interpolation problem with 200 points; it cannot pool information across coordinates the way an additive kernel does, so it is a full-interaction model with the wrong inductive bias on exactly the families where the additive cells have the right one. On interaction_g0.25 the full-interaction bias is right and the oracle behaves like one.

[observation] Two further weaknesses of the MAP reference. First, oracle_S re-queries its own neighbourhood: 24 % (aligned3), 12 % (aligned10), 8 % (decoupled) and 10 % (interaction_g0.25) of its queries at t >= 100 lie within 0.01, in every one of the 100 coordinates, of an earlier query; the four cells and dsp_map are at 0 to 1 % [L88]. [hypothesis] With FilterFeatures the acquisition is exactly flat in the 97 inactive coordinates, so the perturbations of the incumbent that sample_around_best adds (sigma 0.001) beat the raw Sobol candidates and the maximizer returns near-copies of the incumbent, wasting the query. Second, on aligned3 seeds 01 and 08 oracle_S and product-lengthscale end with identical regret to every printed digit (0.2113769952 and 0.0189844172) because neither improved on a point queried at t = 20 (oracle_S, both seeds) and t = 20 / 24 (product-lengthscale) that has all three active coordinates on the box boundary; the two points differ in the inactive coordinates (max difference 0.83 and 1.00) and agree on S exactly [L89]. [hypothesis] Both are ARD Matern models that, at t = 20 with 20 points, are confident enough to send the maximizer to a vertex and then see no expected improvement anywhere else for 180 iterations; the additive cells reached 3e-7 and 7e-6 on seed 01 [L90]. Vertices are not wrong per se: incumbents have 37 to 66 % of their active coordinates at 0 or 1 across all model-based methods [L91], because the family's one-dimensional draws often peak at an edge. The trap is being unable to leave a wrong vertex.

[what a fair oracle would be] The same structural class as the cell under test, fitted on S: for the additive families an additive GP on the true coordinates (additive Matern factors, MAP or NUTS, cheap at |S| <= 10), and for the interaction families a product kernel on S. Cheaper still and arguably fairer: the study's own cells with the prior restricted to S (a_sq or rho fixed at zero off S), which isolates "knowing S" from "having the right kernel". Any of these costs minutes per run; oracle_S took 0.19 h per run on a CPU [L92].

What else looks wrong? narrative §5c

  1. The share threshold is design-dependent (section 3). p_active thresholds a_sq in units of the design-standardized target, and BO inflates that scale by a y_std^2 of 1.8 to 2.7 on three of the four families [L48]. This is a units problem in the readout, not in the cells, and it is fixable offline: multiply a_sq by y_std^2 (recorded per iteration in iterations.csv) before comparing with 0.02, or divide the label shares by y_std^2. [observation plus inference; confidence high]
  2. The additive cells invent a main effect on the interaction family. At t = 199, 6 of 10 additive-lengthscale runs and 5 of 10 additive-amplitude runs on interaction_g0.25 declare one inactive coordinate active (7 and 5 false positives in total; the largest off-S native scores are rho 1.5e5 and a_sq 0.59); the product cells have no false positive on that family, and the additive cells have at most one on any other family [L39]. [hypothesis; medium] The interaction variance an additive model cannot represent is partly absorbed by a spurious one-dimensional component on a free coordinate. This is also the source of additive-lengthscale's falling AP on that family (0.89 at t = 125 to 0.78 at t = 199 [L57]) while its F1 holds: the false positive climbs above a true coordinate in rho without the true ones losing membership.
  3. Interaction seed 08 (section 2): three cells and dsp_map stuck 2.4 below f_star while product-lengthscale and oracle_S found the optimum [L31]. One seed, not evidence of anything by itself, but the seed to look at if a paper wants a failure case for additive models.
  4. dsp_map's fit is fragile at this dimension. 27 iterations raised NotPSDError twice in the MAP fit and queried a seeded random point instead (aligned3 7, aligned10 6, interaction_g0.25 14, decoupled 0), and 92 further iterations survived only through the Adam fallback (aligned3 20, aligned10 10, decoupled 32, interaction_g0.25 30) [L93]. The random-point iterations all occur at t >= 86, i.e. on designs of 100 or more points [L94]. They are rare (0.4 % and 1.3 % of its iterations) and do not change dsp_map's ranking.
  5. Product-lengthscale's per-seed losses on aligned10 are bimodal (section 2, [L37]): a per-seed look at which active coordinates the acquisition stopped moving would be the diagnostic; not done here.

Summary narrative §9

On four synthetic 100-dimensional objectives with 3 to 10 active coordinates, a 2x2 of fully Bayesian sparse GP surrogates (additive or product structure; sparsity on lengthscales or on amplitudes) was compared with quasi-random search, a dimension-scaled-prior MAP GP and an oracle restricted to the true coordinates, over 200 evaluations and 10 paired seeds. The structure-matched cell optimized best in every family, in 10 of 10 seeds against the weaker references, and all cells beat quasi-random search. All four cells ranked the active coordinates near-perfectly by their own sparsity parameter and by Sobol indices, but the thresholded active-set rule under-recalled in the amplitude cells, whose NUTS chains never converged and whose share threshold was applied in design-deflated units. The additive-lengthscale cell showed an intermittent all-active posterior mode after convergence, invisible to single-chain diagnostics. The MAP ARD oracle is a weak reference on additive objectives. Sampler repair and re-scoring precede further runs.
